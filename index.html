    How to study for C2

    Your vocabulary is already strong; the gap to C2 is mostly about control. That means choosing the exact word, building flexible sentences and knowing when an idiom fits. This plan spends your time where that gap is.

    The 80/20 of vocabulary

    Learn chunks, not single words. "Pose a threat" is one item, not two. Examiners notice collocation far more than rare words.

    Core before long tail. Each word list puts its most useful fifth first. Master those across all nine lists before you go deeper into any one of them.

    Use it within 48 hours. Recognising a word is not the same as owning it. Put each new item into a sentence of your own in that day's writing task.

    Review on a schedule. Go back to new items after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days. The word lists do this for you: every item you meet joins a review queue, comes back after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days, and drops back to the start if you miss it. Clear "Review due cards" each day and the schedule takes care of itself.

    Reading Shakespeare without pain

    Read the passage aloud twice: once for sense, once for rhythm. Then read the note. Don't translate word by word; look for the move the speaker makes, such as a contrast, a list of three or a slow build. Free annotated texts are on the Folger Shakespeare Library website (folger.edu), where you can read the whole scene around any passage.

    A week, end to end

    Do days on Monday to Friday, four days per act. On Saturday, redo any drill you missed and take one gap quiz per list. On Sunday, read one long article (a Guardian long read, The Economist, the London Review of Books) and note five chunks from it.

    A full day, about 70 minutes

    • 5 minReview due cards in the word lists
    • 8 minThe passage, read aloud twice, then the note
    • 7 minFour expressions and today's six words: say an example of your own for each
    • 8 minThree drills, answered before you check
    • 15 minThe writing or speaking task, using at least three new items, then Claude's feedback
    • 2 minMark the day done and the words you now own

    Short on time? Drills and writing matter most; the passage can wait for the weekend.
